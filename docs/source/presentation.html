GlucoTwin02
Problem statement

Dangerous lows and highs are often visible in the data before they happen, but that data is split across systems.

The health record (EHR)

Who the patient is

Diabetes type, HbA1c, medications, history of hypoglycaemia.

CGM + wearable

What is happening now

Glucose trend, heart rate, activity, sleep, meals and insulin.

Today's alerts

React, don't anticipate

Clinicians rarely see both sources together, and most CGM alerts react to the current value instead of the next two hours.

Our question

Can a patient digital twin that fuses static EHR context with live sensor streams forecast glucose 2 hours ahead, flag threshold crossings, and show whether each data source actually helps?

GlucoTwin03
Healthcare use case

Decision support for diabetes care teams monitoring patients on CGM

Who and how
  • Users: endocrinologists, diabetes nurses and remote-monitoring teams caring for people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
  • Workflow: sensor data arrives every 5 min → the twin updates → forecast, risk and reasons appear on one screen → the clinician reviews.
  • Value: earlier warning of lows and highs, EHR and sensor context in one view, uncertainty always shown, explanations to check before acting.
  • Safety: informational only; it never gives treatment or insulin-dosing advice.

GlucoTwin04
Our solution

A stateful virtual patient that updates every 5 minutes

GlucoTwin combines each patient's EHR record with their CGM and wearable stream, forecasts the next 2 hours with uncertainty, estimates calibrated low / high risk, and explains every prediction in a doctor-facing dashboard.

DifferentiatorWhat we built
A real twin, not a static chartPatientTwin re-reads only data up to its clock and re-runs every model at each update; a replay shows it evolve live
Measured value of data fusionThree-way ablation (glucose → + wearable → + EHR) with block-bootstrap confidence intervals
Scientific hygieneHeld-out patients and a chronological cut-off with embargo; selection and calibration never touch test data
Honest uncertaintyConformal prediction intervals; risk models refuse to output probabilities when events are too rare
Safety by designWithholds forecasts on stale CGM; labels observed / imputed / simulated data; no dosing advice
Interoperability-readySQLite → PostgreSQL path; FHIR R4-style export with SNOMED-CT / LOINC codes

Technical stack

Pure Python, runs offline on a laptop

Language & data
  • Python 3.10+ (tested on 3.14)
  • pandas, NumPy, SciPy
  • SQLite, PostgreSQL-ready
  • FHIR R4-style JSON, CSV
Machine learning
  • XGBoost (gradient boosting)
  • scikit-learn: ridge, isotonic calibration
  • Split-conformal intervals
  • TreeSHAP explanations
App & quality
  • Streamlit dashboard
  • Plotly charts
  • pytest: causality and leakage tests
  • joblib artifacts, fingerprinted
Data sources

Synthetic cohort generator; importers for OhioT1DM XML and generic CSV; GlucoFM-Bench via Hugging Face datasets.

Speed

Data generation ≈ 6 s · full training ≈ 30 s · one twin update ≈ 0.3 s.

Dependencies

No paid APIs, no cloud. XGBoost is optional, with an automatic fallback to scikit-learn.

GlucoTwin08
Evaluation design

Every reported number comes from patients and times the models never saw

Chronological cut-off at 70% of each timeline · dashed = 2-hour embargo
01 · Patients

Test patients are never used for training, model selection or calibration.

02 · Time

Chronological cut-off with a 2-hour embargo so no target leaks across it.

03 · Preprocessing

Imputers, scalers and calibrators are fitted on training data only.

04 · Tests

Automated tests check causal features, no patient overlap and the embargo.

How to read this honestly
  • Wearable signals clearly help the forecast; EHR context adds little once wearable data is present.
  • Lows are rare (125 test windows); adding static EHR features made the hypo model worse, consistent with overfitting on 30 training patients.
  • Interval coverage is 0.76–0.81 against a nominal 0.80.
  • Synthetic results show the pipeline works; they are not clinical evidence.

Safety, risks & limitations

What we guard against, and what remains open

Risk / limitationMitigation in this prototypeNeeded next
Synthetic data encodes our assumptionsEvery assumption listed in-app; results framed as a pipeline demonstrationExternal validation on real CGM + EHR data
Data leakagePatient + chronological split, 2-h embargo, train-only preprocessing, testsProspective validation
Rare hypo eventsMinimum-event rules; refuse to show probabilities; report base ratesLarger cohorts, event-level metrics
Missing / stale sensor dataExplicit flags; gaps > 30 min never imputed; forecast withheld if CGM > 15 min oldDevice-level quality signals
Over-trust in explanationsLabelled as associations; method named on screenClinician user studies
Privacy, bias, regulationSynthetic only; research positioning; no dosing adviceDe-identification, subgroup audits, V&V and regulatory review
Not a medical device14
Outcomes & next steps

What we delivered, and where it goes next

Delivered
  • End-to-end digital twin: data → features → models → twin → dashboard
  • 2-h MAE 20.6 mg/dL vs 31.9 for persistence on unseen patients
  • Measured fusion gain: wearable data helps significantly; EHR gain not significant, reported as such
  • Calibrated risk with refusal, conformal intervals, TreeSHAP reasons
  • Real-data importers and FHIR-style export ready
Next
  • Validate on real CGM data (OhioT1DM under its data-use agreement, or a clinical partner)
  • Subgroup and fairness audits on representative data
  • Hybrid mechanistic + ML twin
  • Event-level alert metrics: lead time, false alerts per day
  • Usability sessions with clinicians; FHIR-native EHR and streaming ingestion
